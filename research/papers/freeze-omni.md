## TL;DR

- **Text LLM frozen.** Speech goes in through encoder + adapter + a trainable prompt embedding. Speech comes out through a small (120M) AR token decoder conditioned on the LLM's text tokens and hidden states (p.2–7).
- **Small data budget:** 60k multi-round Q&A (answers regenerated by the backbone itself), 110k h of ASR, ~3k h of TTS-generated pairs, on 8 GPUs (p.1, p.6–7).
- **Spoken QA lands close to the backbone's text QA:** Web Q 44.73 vs 45.13, LLaMA Q 72.0 vs 77.67, TriviaQA 53.88 vs 63.93, scored on the model's *text* output (p.9, Tab.3).
- **Talker conditioning matters.** Conditioning the speech decoder on LLM hidden states, plus a small pre-network, cuts CER from 5.27 to 2.19 at top-k=1 (p.8, Tab.2).
- **Duplex is VAD plus a 3-way state head.** Measured latency from the turn switch to first audio is about 745 ms; the authors estimate about 1.2 s in real use (p.8–9).

## Architecture

- **Input:** streaming encoder → adapter → frozen LLM. Downsampling cuts prefill cost and latency (p.3).
- **Output:** sentence-split chunks of LLM text tokens go to the NAR speech decoder. LLM hidden states go to a NAR *prefix* decoder whose kv-cache feeds the NAR decoder. The AR decoder writes speech tokens into a FIFO for the codec (p.3, p.5).
- **State head:** predicts only on the last frame of each encoder chunk, during prefill. Both "ended" states stop the audio input and reset the VAD (p.6, Fig.4).
- **Serving:** a "model as a server" pool of instances with per-user caches (p.6).

## Training pipeline

The LLM is frozen in every stage. Batch sizes, step counts, GPU type and hours are not reported; the only compute statement is that all experiments ran on 8 GPUs (p.7).

### Stage 0: tokenizer / codec / decoder training
- TiCodec on speech only, customized to a single codebook of 1,024 entries at 40 Hz, with TiCodec's own hyperparameters (p.5, p.7).
- A single codebook is described as sufficient for a limited number of speakers (p.5).

### Stage 1 (input): encoder ASR pretraining
CTC loss on the encoder alone, using 110k h of internal Chinese + English ASR; LR 2e-4 (p.4, p.6–7).

### Stage 2 (input): encoder–LLM alignment
- **Trainable:** encoder, adapter and some special tokens. The LLM is frozen.
- **Target:** the transcript, on the same 110k h, with dynamic-chunk training.
- **LR:** 1e-4 (p.4, p.6–7).

### Stage 3 (input): speech-in Q&A plus the state head
- **Trainable:** one prompt embedding, shared before every question, plus the state classifier.
- **Frozen:** the encoder and the LLM.
- **Data:** 60k multi-round Q&A with TTS'd questions and backbone-generated answers.
- **Loss:** multi-task cross-entropy on the LLM output and the state labels.
- **LR:** 6e-4 (p.4, p.6–7).

### Stage 2 (output): text → speech tokens
- NAR and AR decoders, sharing parameters, are fed the outputs of the LLM's frozen embedding layer. Teacher forcing on codec labels.
- **Data:** ~3k h of TTS-generated pairs. **LR:** 5e-5 (p.5, p.7).

### Stage 3 (output): prefix fine-tune on LLM hidden states
- Only a new NAR prefix decoder is trained.
- **Data:** the same 60k Q&A. Labels are made by TTS-ing the LLM's own text outputs and codec-tokenizing them.
- **Purpose:** couple the decoder to the style of LLM-written text, which differs from generic TTS text, and so reduce bad cases. **LR:** 5e-5 (p.5–7).

## Data pipeline

- **Q&A:** 60,000 random multi-round dialogues from moss-003-sft-data. The answers are replaced with Qwen2-7B-Instruct's own, so the targets match the backbone's own output distribution (p.4, p.6).
- **TTS:** a zero-shot / multi-speaker TTS for questions. Output speech is studied in the single-speaker case (p.4, p.6–7).
- **Other data:** 110k h of internal ASR (p.6); ~3k h of zero-shot-TTS text–speech pairs (p.7).
- **Not described:** how the state-1 vs state-2 duplex labels were built.

## Preserving text intelligence / knowledge

**Mechanism.** The LLM is never updated. Only the input-side encoder, adapter and prompt embedding learn; the output-side decoder reads LLM states without back-propagating into the LLM (p.2–6).

**What it preserves.** Speech-in/text-out QA versus text-in QA on the same backbone, with Moshi vs its own Helium backbone from the same table for comparison (Tab.3, p.9):

| Benchmark | Freeze-Omni speech | Qwen2-7B-Instruct text | gap | Moshi speech | Helium text | gap |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Web Q | 44.73 | 45.13 | −0.4 | 26.6 | 32.3 | −5.7 |
| LLaMA Q | 72.0 | 77.67 | −5.7 | 62.3 | 75 | −12.7 |
| TriviaQA | 53.88 | 63.93 | −10.1 | 22.8 | 56.4 | −33.6 |

(Gaps derived.) GLM-4-Voice in the same table: 32.2 / 64.7 / 39.1. Prompt following and role-play are kept; changing the response style requires text fine-tuning of the LLM (p.2).

**What it costs, per the paper:**
1. The remaining gap comes entirely from the speech-input path, and it grows on harder knowledge QA (TriviaQA −10 points).
2. A good encoder needs 110k h of ASR.
3. The output is a single voice with no style control, evaluated only by CER (p.7).
4. Duplex is not native: VAD-gated, no backchannels, no speaking while listening.
5. QA is scored on the *text* output, so whether knowledge survives into the speech is not measured (Tab.3 caption, p.9).

## Key results

- **ASR, input-stage-2 encoder** (Tab.1, p.8):
  - Non-streaming (chunk=∞): aishell-1 CER 2.15; LibriSpeech test-clean / test-other WER 3.24 / 7.68.
  - Final streaming setting (fixed chunk=4): 2.48 and 3.82 / 9.79.
- **Speech-decoder CER** (Tab.2, p.8): 1,000 LLM outputs scored with paraformer-zh; ranges are over top-k = 1–5:
  - no prefix: 4.64–5.27
  - prefix: 3.53–3.9
  - prefix + pre-net: 1.69–2.19
- **Latency** (Tab.4, p.9), from the turn switch to the first PCM chunk: average 745 ms, median 753, p90 1,020. Breakdown:
  - 478 ms LLM to first text chunk
  - 15 ms speech-decoder prefill
  - 237 ms to first speech-token chunk
  - 11 ms codec decode

  On top of this, endpoint detection adds ~160–320 ms and the network ~200–300 ms, giving about 1.2 s in practice (p.8–9).

## Ablations & lessons

- **Condition the talker on LLM hidden states, not just text.** The prefix kv-cache brings CER from 5.27 to 3.9 at top-k=1, because LLM-written text differs in style from generic TTS text (p.6–8, Tab.2).
- **Give the decoder capacity to map LLM embeddings.** An extra 2-layer pre-network: 3.9 → 2.19 (Tab.2).
- **Streaming encoder:** training with a fixed chunk beats dynamic-chunk training at the same chunk size (test-other 9.79 vs 10.48), but locks the chunk size (p.7).
- **Answers regenerated by the frozen backbone** keep training targets inside the backbone's own distribution (p.4). Not ablated.
- **No frozen-vs-fine-tuned LLM ablation.** The "same intelligence" claim rests on Tab.3 alone.

## Implications for our setup

- **If knowledge in speech is the priority,** the evidence favours keeping the text LLM (nearly) untouched, putting the speech burden on the input encoder, and using a separate talker that reads LLM hidden states. That is the opposite of mid-training the whole Qwen3 LLM on duplex audio. Middle path: freeze or LoRA the backbone in early duplex training, and/or keep a text-only stream in the loss.
- **Self-distill SFT targets.** Regenerate the text targets of any SFT/QA data we synthesize with our own Qwen3 backbone, so the speech model learns to say what the LLM would say (p.4).
- **If a talker module generates our audio,** condition it on hidden states and not only on text tokens (Tab.2).
- **Diagnostic for our current model:** TTS the Web Q / LLaMA Q / TriviaQA questions and compare speech-in vs text-in accuracy on our backbone, as Tab.3 does. This separates loss of input understanding from loss of output generation. Also ASR-score the spoken answers, which Freeze-Omni did not do.
- **Not a template for natural duplex.** It is VAD-gated turn switching with no backchannels or overlap.

## Open questions / unclear points

- How the state labels were built and how much data trained the state head.
- Batch sizes, steps, GPU type and GPU-hours.
- Whether any real (non-TTS) or noisy speech was used in input stage 3. Web Q and TriviaQA were TTS'd with edge-tts for evaluation (p.8).
- No speech-output quality metrics beyond CER.
