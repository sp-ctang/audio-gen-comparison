## TL;DR

- An encoder–adapter–decoder audio-understanding family: a from-scratch 0.6B encoder at 12.5 Hz, DeepStack multi-layer feature injection, and explicit elapsed-time markers every 2 s (p.4–5).
- **Pretraining uses ~1.2T tokens with a default mixture of 30% ASR-related, 40% audio captioning and 30% text-only LM.** The text-only data is switched on only in Stage 2, when the LLM is unfrozen (p.9).
- Post-training runs SFT, which yields the Instruct models; then a reasoning cold start (audio plus text-only reasoning) and DAPO RL, which yield the Thinking models (p.9–12).
- MOSS-Audio-8B-Thinking averages **71.08** over MMAU, MMAU-Pro, MMAR and MMSU, the best open model in their table (Tab.1). 8B-Instruct reaches an average ASR CER of 11.30 and timestamp-ASR shifts of 35.77 ms on AISHELL-1 and 131.61 ms on LibriSpeech (Tab.3–4).
- No text-benchmark results, compute, LR or batch sizes are reported, so text preservation is asserted but never measured.

## Architecture

Covered in the header. In addition: both adapters share the GatedMLP design. DeepStack adds intermediate encoder states to "selected early layers" of the decoder (p.4–5). Time markers ("2", "4", …) are embedded jointly with the audio features (p.5). The encoder's local attention deliberately leaves "long-range semantic reasoning" to the LLM (p.4).

## Training pipeline

### Stage 0: tokenizer / codec / decoder training
The encoder is trained from scratch on "millions of hours" with ASR, AST and audio-caption tasks (p.4). The decoder used for that training, plus steps and compute, are not reported.

### Stage 1: pretraining, alignment
"Mainly" the adapter and DeepStack modules train, while "the audio encoder and language model are kept relatively stable"; whether they are frozen or on a low LR is not stated. **The mix is audio–text only** (ASR-related tasks plus captioning), with no text-only data (p.9).

### Stage 2: pretraining, full model
The encoder, adapter, DeepStack and LLM all train end to end. Mixture: 30% ASR-related (plain ASR, word-level and sentence-level timestamp ASR), 40% merged audio captions, and **30% text-only pretraining text** without instruction formatting (math, code, education, literature, general). Within each group, datasets are sampled in proportion to √size. Pretraining totals ≈1.2T tokens (p.9). The stage split, LR, batch and sequence length are not reported.

### Stage 3: SFT, producing the Instruct models
The mix covers audio QA generated by LMs from speech, music and general captions; caption tasks; ASR and timestamp ASR; and self-identity data (p.9–10). Size and hyperparameters are not reported.

### Stage 4: reasoning cold start
Audio-centred reasoning traces plus **text-only reasoning data**, included "to transfer general reasoning patterns from the text modality" (p.10).

### Stage 5: DAPO RL, producing the Thinking models (p.10–12)
- **Rollouts:** temperature 1.0, top-p 1.0, top-k 50; 16 samples per prompt; rollout batch 128; max response 2,048 tokens; 160 rollouts per training round.
- **Objective:** clip ε 0.2 (low) and 0.28 (high); token-level importance-sampling correction (TIS threshold 2.0, lower bound 0.0); dynamic filtering of zero-variance groups.
- **Rewards:** correctness, response quality, format, and "usefulness of the reasoning process". Reward models are not described.
- **Run length:** about 140 steps, read from the Fig.4–5 axes.
- **Dynamics:**
  - Reward EMA rises from ~0.69 to >0.82 (maximum raw 0.847 at step 136).
  - Response length falls to 171 tokens at step 12, peaks at 334 at step 33, then settles at 250–270.
  - Extra over-sampling rounds needed to refill filtered batches grow from 1 to 14 by step 139.

## Data pipeline

- **Segmentation.** Wild audio (audiobooks, podcasts, broadcast, film, online content) is cut at event boundaries found by BEATs-based sound-event detection, rather than in fixed windows. Each segment is routed to speech, music or general-audio annotation branches by its event profile (p.6).
- **ASR labels.** Ensemble pseudo-labels from Qwen3-Omni, FunASR-Nano and Qwen3-ASR, keeping only segments with low cross-system WER. Language ID is cross-checked, and MMS_FA alignment supplies word and sentence timestamps (p.6).
- **Captions.** Speech captions are speaker-aware (DiariZen plus a bootstrapped captioner). General-audio dense captions come from fusing SED events with Qwen3-Omni models, plus an LLM judge and synthetic overlap mixtures. Music captions are built from MIR tool outputs. All branches are merged by a router and two-step LLM synthesis into one caption per clip (p.6–8).
- **Scale:** "millions of hours" (p.3). Per-task hours are not reported.

## Preserving text intelligence / knowledge

- 30% text-only pretraining data is mixed in **only once the LLM is unfrozen** (Stage 2). It is meant to prevent degradation of "fluent generation, knowledge expression, reasoning over text, and code understanding" (p.9).
- Text-only reasoning data is included in the cold start (p.10).
- **No text benchmarks are reported**, so the effect of the 30% replay is unknown.

## Key results

- **General audio understanding** (Tab.1): MOSS-Audio-8B-Thinking scores MMAU 77.33, MMAU-Pro 64.92, MMAR 66.53 and MMSU 75.52, an average of **71.08**. Averages for comparison:
  - MOSS-Audio-8B-Instruct 66.32; MOSS-Audio-4B-Thinking 68.37
  - Qwen3-Omni-30B-A3B-Instruct 67.91; Step-Audio-R1 70.67
  - Gemini-3.1-Pro 79.89

- **Speech captioning** (13 dimensions, LLM-judged): 8B-Instruct 3.725 against Gemini-3.1-Pro 3.577 and Qwen3-Omni-Instruct 3.599 (Tab.2).
- **ASR** (12 dimensions, average CER): 8B-Instruct 11.30 against Qwen3-Omni-Instruct 11.39 (Tab.3).
- **Timestamp ASR shift (AAS)**, AISHELL-1 / LibriSpeech: 35.77 / 131.61 ms against Qwen3-Omni-Instruct 833.66 / 646.95 ms (Tab.4).

## Ablations & lessons

- **Encoder quality** (XARES-LLM framework, Tab.6):
  - Understanding average: MOSS 0.760 against Whisper-large-v3 0.743 and Qwen3-Omni's AuT 0.809 (AuT is better here).
  - Speech and generation average: MOSS 0.673 against AuT 0.661 and Whisper 0.492.
- **Controlled ASR comparison** (each encoder with Qwen3-1.7B, same pretraining data, 100k steps): average error 16.31 for MOSS against 17.61 for AuT across 38 sets, with MOSS better on 36. Whispered speech improves from 12.45 to 7.87 (Tab.7).
- **DeepStack gives an almost-zero overall gain.** With Qwen3-0.6B-base, MECAT-Caption DATE goes from 0.4823 to 0.4831. Speech categories get worse (pure 0.4861→0.4791) while music and sound improve (music 0.4705→0.4927, pure sound 0.4015→0.4255) (Tab.8). Intermediate layers help non-speech cues at a small cost to speech.
- **Thinking variants beat Instruct on understanding** (71.08 vs 66.32 at 8B). The paper positions Instruct for transcription and captioning, but only Instruct models are reported on those tasks (p.3, p.12).
- **RL dynamics.** Dynamic filtering is needed because more and more groups become all-correct; over-sampling cost rises to 14 extra rounds per step. Reward improves without longer responses (Fig.4–5).
- Time markers and timestamp supervision coincide with much lower AAS than omni baselines. This was not ablated in isolation (Tab.4).

## Implications for our setup

- **Text replay data point.** MOSS used 30% text tokens while unfreezing the LLM, but never measured text ability. Audex shows 44–69% replay with measured preservation, so treat 30% as a lower bound.
- **Stage pattern matches Audex.** Open the audio pathway with adapters first while the LLM is (nearly) fixed, with no text needed; then unfreeze everything and add text replay.
- **√size sampling within data groups** is a simple heuristic for mixing heterogeneous speech sources, e.g. duplex corpora of very different sizes.
- **For the listening side of a streaming model,** a 12.5 Hz encoder with 8 s sliding-window attention is designed for KV-cached streaming. Explicit time markers every 2 s are a cheap way to give the LLM absolute timing. Our extrapolation, untested for duplex: this could help turn-timing.
- **RL recipe.** If we later run RL on audio-understanding or spoken-QA outputs in text, the DAPO settings above (16 samples, clip 0.2/0.28, dynamic filtering) are a ready starting point.
- **Nothing here addresses speech generation or audio continuation.**

## Open questions / unclear points

- Which LLMs initialize the 4B and 8B models, and which encoder and decoder layers DeepStack connects.
- What "kept relatively stable" means in Stage 1 (frozen, or low LR?).
- How the 1.2T tokens split across stages; LR, batch size and steps for pretraining and SFT; data hours per task.
- The meaning of "rollout batch 128" vs "160 rollouts per training round"; the RL data size and reward models.
- With no text-only evaluation, whether the 30% replay actually preserved the LLM's knowledge.
